  ROOT-CAUSE ANALYSIS: WHY DID WER INCREASE FROM 35.1% → 54.7%?
========================================================================

  FACTOR 1 — Test set is harder (more speakers, lower per-speaker data)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Pilot:     38 samples, 1 speaker (ashaheble), all Story 5
  50-epoch: 104 samples, 3 speakers — includes dipti (3 train samples!)
    ashaheble: 156 train / 35 test → mean WER 0.517
    dipti.ajgaonkar21: 3 train / 35 test → mean WER 0.603
    lalimomadi: 125 train / 34 test → mean WER 0.519

  FACTOR 2 — Overfitting (all 119M encoder params updated on 511 samples)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  On matched samples (same audio, same speaker — ashaheble):
    Pilot mean WER:    0.3511
    50-epoch mean WER: 0.4953
    Change:            +14.4pp  (REGRESSION on same speaker same data)
  Even for ashaheble (dominant train speaker), WER regressed — 
  strong evidence that the longer full-encoder training hurt generalisation.
  Val loss doubled (epoch 6→49), train_loss collapsed to 0.17.
  Pilot stopped at epoch 20 (best at ep 13) — less overfitting.

  FACTOR 3 — Severe speaker imbalance for dipti
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  dipti.ajgaonkar21: 3 train samples, 35 test samples → WER 0.603
  Model was almost never trained on this voice → poor generalisation.

  Overfitting (encoder, 50 ep)     ~+8–12pp (ashaheble WER 35→52%)
  Test set harder (3 speakers)     Interaction of the above
